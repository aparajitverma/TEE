2.1 SOURCING THE THREE FRUITS
Step	Action	Practical Tips
Identify growers	• Use APEDA‑registered fruit growers, state horticulture corporations (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh).
• Look for Amla (Phyllanthus emblica), Haritaki (Terminalia chebula), Bibhitaki (Terminalia bellirica) orchards that practice good agricultural practices (GAP).	Prefer organic‑certified farms (USDA‑NOP / EU‑EURO+) if you plan the organic route.
Supply agreement	Define quantity, moisture ≤ 12 %, grade (size, color), price, delivery schedule, penalties for contamination. Include traceability clause (lot‑number → farmer).	Secure multiple source contracts (≥ 2 farms per fruit) to mitigate supply risk.
Harvest & post‑harvest handling	• Harvest at optimal ripeness (Amla ≈ 6‑8 weeks, Haritaki/Bibhitaki ≈ 8‑10 weeks).
• Shade‑dry under well‑ventilated conditions; keep temperature ≤ 45 °C to preserve vitamin C and polyphenols.	Store dried fruit in jute bags with desiccant packets, in a cool (≤ 25 °C), low‑humidity warehouse.
First‑level QC at farm gate	• Visual inspection, moisture test, random HPLC for total polyphenols (target ≥ 10 % in Amla, ≥ 5 % in Haritaki/Bibhitaki).	Use a portable UV‑Vis for quick screening; confirm with lab for a proportion (≈ 5 % of batch).
Lead time: 7‑10 days from contract signing to raw fruit arrival at your processing unit.

2.2 PROCESSING & VALUE‑ADDITION
Process	Options	Recommended set‑up (sourcing model)
Cleaning & size reduction	Mechanical cleaning, removal of foreign matter, dry‑grinding (30‑50 mm → 2‑5 mm).	Outsource to a GMP‑certified fruit‐processing unit (e.g., Kadi/ Gujarat processing hub).
Drying	• Sun‑dry under shade (traditional).
• Hot‑air dryer (45‑55 °C, 8‑12 h).
• Freeze‑dry (premium, retains bio‑actives).	Hot‑air dryer gives consistent moisture < 10 % with moderate cost; freeze‑dry for a premium organic line.
Milling to fine powder	Hammer mill → sieve (80‑100 µm).	Ensure particle‑size uniformity for capsule filling.
Blending	Standard ratio 1 : 1 : 1 (Amla : Haritaki : Bibhitaki) by weight; can adjust for polyphenol‑enriched blends (e.g., 40 % Amla).	Use automated loss‑in‑weight blender with batch‑recorded lot numbers.
Standardization (optional)	• Total polyphenol content ≥ 30 % (spectrophotometric Folin‑Ciocalteu).
• Vitamin C ≥ 200 mg/100 g (HPLC).	If targeting premium nutraceutical markets, standardize to 30 % polyphenols and label accordingly.
Final product formats	1. Bulk powder (10 kg‑50 kg bags).
2. Capsules (300 mg or 500 mg; gelatin or vegan HPMC).
3. Loose‑leaf tea sachets (1‑g dosed).
4. Ready‑to‑drink (RTD) sachets (powder + sweetener).	Start with capsules (most accepted) and bulk powder for B2B; later add tea/RTD for consumer DTC channels.
Packaging	• HDPE/PP bottles (250 g, 500 g) for powder.
• Aluminum/PP blister packs (30‑60 caps).
• Eco‑friendly kraft sachets (for tea).	Choose tamper‑evident, recyclable packaging to meet “clean‑label” expectations.
Typical processing duration: 2‑3 weeks from raw fruit receipt to sealed finished product (including QC).

2.3 CERTIFICATIONS & COMPLIANCE
Certification	Issuer	Why You Need It
GMP (Food / Nutraceutical)	FSSAI + AYUSH‑GMP	Mandatory for US/EU supplement importers.
ISO 9001 / ISO 22000	Accredited bodies (SGS, BSI, TÜV)	Demonstrates consistent quality & food‑safety management.
Organic (USDA‑NOP / EU‑EURO+)	NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production) + accredited certifiers (Control Union, Ecocert)	Opens premium “organic” niche (price premium 20‑40 %).
Vegan / Vegetarian (optional)	Vegan Society, EU‑Vegan certification	Important for vegan markets (US, EU, Australia).
Halal (optional, Gulf)	JAKIM, IFANCA	Required for many Middle‑East distributors.
Certificate of Analysis (CoA)	In‑house or third‑party lab (CSIR‑IICT, Intertek, Eurofins)	Shows polyphenol % and safety (heavy metals < 10 ppm, pesticides < MRL).
Certificate of Origin	Chamber of Commerce (e.g., Gujarat Chamber)	Needed for customs duties, may enable GSP preferential rates.
Phytosanitary Certificate	Plant Quarantine, Ministry of Agriculture	Confirms product is pest‑free; required by many countries.
Export License / IEC	DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade)	Basic legal requirement for any export from India.
If you contract a processing plant that already holds GMP, ISO, and organic, you can avoid duplicate audits—just verify that the certificates are current and cover the exact product you will market.

Typical timelines:

GMP/ISO audit: 30‑45 days
Organic certification: 60‑90 days (field inspection)
Halal: 30‑45 days

QR‑code (optional)	Link to online CoA, storage instructions, third‑party test results – a trust‑building tool for DTC platforms.
2.6 LOGISTICS & FREIGHT
Mode	When to Use	Key Points
Sea freight (FCL)	Bulk shipments > 10 tons.	Use dry‑air‑conditioned containers (temperature ≤ 25 °C) to protect moisture‑sensitive powder.
Sea freight (LCL)	Smaller orders (< 5 tons).	Consolidate with other Indian nutraceutical exporters to reduce cost.
Air freight	High‑value, urgent orders (< 5 tons).	Faster customs clearance; pack in sealed, tamper‑evident boxes.
Insurance	All shipments.	Insure for CIF value + 10 %; for premium organic batches, add all‑risk cargo insurance.
Customs clearance	Destination country.	Use an experienced customs broker (e.g., Kuehne + Nagel, Expeditors) familiar with HS code 210690 (herbal preparations).
Typical lead times:

India → US (sea): 30‑45 days + 5‑7 days customs.
India → EU (sea): 35‑50 days.
Air: 5‑7 days door‑to‑door.

3.3 Sales Funnel & Closing Steps
Initial Contact – Send sample kit (30 g powder + CoA) + price quotation (INCOTERMS: FOB India).
Technical Evaluation – Answer R&D queries: polyphenol assay method, residual solvent (if any), heavy‑metal limits, organic certification.
Negotiation – Discuss MOQ (2‑5 tons for first order), price (e.g., $18 / kg FOB organic), payment terms (30 % T/T upfront, 70 % against B/L).
Contract – Draft a Purchase Agreement covering: product specs, QC acceptance, warranty (≤ 5 % variance), force‑majeure, arbitration (ICC).
Production & QC – Run pilot batch, obtain batch CoA, share digital copy with buyer for pre‑shipment approval.
Shipping – Forward BL, Commercial Invoice, Packing List, CO, Phytosanitary Cert to buyer’s freight forwarder.
Post‑sale – Provide technical support (formulation tips), ask for feedback, request testimonials. Offer re‑order discount (5‑10 % for repeat within 6 months).

6️⃣ RISK MANAGEMENT & CONTINGENCIES
Risk	Likelihood	Impact	Mitigation
Raw‑fruit shortage (climate/pest)	Medium	High (production halt)	• Diversify sourcing across 2‑3 states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh).
• Keep 12‑month buffer stock of dried fruit.
Regulatory change (e.g., stricter heavy‑metal limits)	Low‑Medium	Medium‑High	• Subscribe to RegDesk or EU‑FSC updates.
• Test every batch for all 7 heavy metals & pesticide residues.
Batch failure (polyphenol variance)	Low	High (rejection, reputation)	• Implement in‑process QC (sample every 200 kg).
• Use Statistical Process Control (SPC) to keep variance < 3 %.
Logistics delay (port congestion, customs hold)	Medium	Medium	• Partner with a reliable freight forwarder with customs brokerage.
• Obtain pre‑clearance (US FDA prior notice).
Currency fluctuation (INR/USD)	Medium	Medium	• Hedge large orders via forward contracts.
• Quote in USD with a price‑adjustment clause (> 5 % INR movement).
IP copy‑cats (e.g., “organic Triphala” clones)	Medium	Low‑Medium	• Register trademark for your brand in target markets.
• If using proprietary standardisation (e.g., 30 % polyphenols), secure licensing agreement with clear IP clauses.

9️⃣ FINAL THOUGHTS
Triphala offers high profit margins because a low‑cost raw fruit blend can be transformed into a premium, standardized, organic or vegan product that commands $12‑$35 / kg FOB (or $3‑$6 per capsule).
Value‑addition (drying, blending, polyphenol standardisation, organic/vegan certification) dramatically lifts price and opens premium market segments.
Secure GMP & ISO certification, and obtain organic/halal/vegan where relevant—these are the currencies of trust for US, EU, and Gulf buyers.
Robust QC (polyphenol assay, heavy‑metal & pesticide testing) and transparent CoA are non‑negotiable for repeat business.
Diversify sales channels: B2B private‑label for nutraceutical manufacturers, DTC e‑commerce for health‑conscious consumers, and Gulf halal distributors for premium pricing.
Follow the 90‑day launch plan, keep the checklist up‑to‑date, and continuously monitor regulatory trends and market demand—you’ll be well‑positioned to turn Indian Triphala into a profitable export commodity.
