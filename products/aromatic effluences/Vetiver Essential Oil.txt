Vetiver Essential Oil


Vetiver (Chrysopogon zizanioides) Essential‑Oil Export Guide – India
“From farm to fragrance client” – a step‑by‑step play‑book for Indian exporters

Target Audience – Small‑/medium‑scale producers, contract manufacturers, and export‑households that already have a distillation capacity or plan to contract it out.

Scope – Covers the complete value‑chain: agronomic sourcing → processing → quality assurance → export‑compliance → freight & delivery.

1. Pre‑Export Readiness (Strategic Foundations)
Action	Why It Matters	How to Do It
Export‑Import Code (IEC) – Obtain via DGFT portal	Mandatory for any foreign‑trade transaction	Apply on DGFT – DGFTWeb (no fee). Keep IEC number handy for all filings.
Registration with APEDA (Agricultural & Processed Food Products Export Development Authority)	Enables access to export incentives, market development grants, and APEDA‑certified quality‑assurance schemes (e.g., APEDA‑Organic).	Submit online through APEDA Exporter’s Registration – upload plant‑site licence, GST, PAN, IEC, and bank details.
Bank Sanction & Letter‑of‑Credit (LC) Facility	Secures payment & reduces foreign‑exchange risk.	Engage your bank to open an export‑advise LC facility. Negotiate LC terms (sight, usance, partial draw‑down).
Product Classification (HS‑Code)	Determines duty, statistical reporting & customs procedures.	Vetiver essential oil – HS 3301.29.00 (essential oils, other). Confirm with Customs.
Market‑Specific Regulatory Intelligence	Each destination (EU, US, GCC, Japan, Australia, etc.) imposes distinct cosmetics/aromatics/food‑additive rules.	Build a Regulatory Matrix – list target market, governing body (e.g., FDA‑CFR, EU‑Cosmetics‑Regulation, REACH, JSO), required safety data (COA, MSDS, CITES status). Update quarterly.
2. Agronomic Sourcing & Sustainable Cultivation
Step	Technical Detail	Best‑Practice Checklist
Varietal Selection	Use certified ‘Rajasthan’ or ‘Kerala’ Vetiver cultivars known for high sesquiterpene‑rich oil (β‑Caryophyllene, Vetivenes, α‑Vetiverene).	Obtain seed/planting material from ICAR‑VRI or a GAAP‑certified nursery.
Soil & Nutrient Management	Vetiver thrives on well‑drained loam, pH 6.0‑7.5. Apply N‑PK‑S based on soil‑test (e.g., 40‑20‑60 kg ha⁻¹).	Conduct soil profile analysis (pH, EC, organic matter) before planting.
Integrated Pest Management (IPM)	Minimize pesticide residues – essential for “clean‑oil”. Use biological controls (Trichoderma spp.) and cultural practices (inter‑cropping).	Keep a Pesticide Use Register; maintain residue‑free status per FSSAI limits.
Harvest Timing	Roots are harvested 12–18 months after planting for optimum oil yield (≈ 0.5–0.8 % w/w).	Schedule a harvest calendar; align with distillery capacity.
Traceability	Assign a Batch‑Lot ID (e.g., VET‑IN‑YYMM‑NN) linked to GPS‑tagged farm.	Use QR‑code on root bundles for downstream traceability.
3. Root Processing & Distillation
Process	Technical Parameters	Quality Controls
Root Cleaning & Grading	Wash with potable water, remove soil, sort by diameter (≥ 15 mm) to ensure uniform mass transfer.	Visual inspection & moisture content (target ≤ 10 % w.b.).
Pre‑Drying (Optional)	Low‑temperature air‑dry (40 °C, 6‑8 h) reduces water load, improves oil concentration.	Moisture meter; record kiln log.
Distillation Unit	Steam distillation preferred (hydro‑distillation for higher sesquiterpene yield). Typical parameters: 1.2 MPa pressure, 95‑100 °C steam, residence time 3‑4 h.	Temperature & pressure sensors calibrated; Steam flow meter verification.
Oil Collection	Use stainless‑steel separators (AISI‑304), maintain inert nitrogen blanket to prevent oxidation.	De‑gassing test – verify no residual water.
Yield Benchmark	0.45‑0.70 % w/w oil from fresh root mass.	Yield logbook – compare against baseline; flag deviations > 10 % for root quality review.
4. Laboratory Quality Assurance (QA) & Certification
Action	Standard / Method	Acceptance Criteria
Gas Chromatography‑Mass Spectrometry (GC‑MS)	ISO 11024‑1:2022 (Essential oils – GC‑MS profiling).	Major constituents: Vetiverol (≥ 30 %), α‑Vetiverene (≥ 3 %). Total α‑Vetiverene + β‑Caryophyllene ≥ 12 %.
Optical Rotation (Polarimetry)	ISO 9366‑1 (Specific rotation).	Specific rotation: [α]D²⁰ = –15 ± 2° (typical range).
Acid Value (AV)	ISO 12966‑2.	AV ≤ 4 mg KOH g⁻¹.
Peroxide Value (PV)	ISO 3960.	PV ≤ 10 meq O₂ kg⁻¹.
Microbial Load (Total Plate Count)	ISO 20776‑1.	≤ 10³ CFU g⁻¹.
Residue Testing (Pesticides, Heavy Metals)	FSSAI & EU MRL limits; use LC‑MS/MS for pesticides, ICP‑MS for metals.	All below statutory MRLs (e.g., Pb ≤ 0.1 ppm).
Certificates	Certificate of Analysis (CoA), Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS), Organic/ISO‑9001/ISO‑14001 if applicable.	Issued by an NABL‑accredited lab (e.g., CSIR‑IICT, NIF). Include batch‑specific GC‑MS chromatogram.
Tip: Maintain a Digital QA Dashboard (ERP integration) that auto‑populates CoA fields upon lab upload, reducing manual errors.

5. Packaging & Preservation
Requirement	Specification	Rationale
Primary Container	Amber‑glass (30 ml, 100 ml, 250 ml) or HDPE (500 ml – 1 l) with PTFE‑lined screw caps.	UV protection (UV‑A/B) and barrier against oxidation; compliant with ISO 16128‑1 for cosmetics.
Secondary Packaging	Four‑wall corrugated cardboard (Ø 300 mm), inner poly‑foam inserts.	Shock‑absorption for road/sea handling.
Atmosphere Control	Nitrogen flush (95 % N₂, 5 % CO₂) before sealing; silica gel packets (1 % wt.) for humidity.	Extends shelf‑life; meets ISO 16448‑1 packaging integrity.
Labeling	Must display: Product name, batch‑lot, net weight, manufacturing date, expiry (24 months from bottling), IN‑PD‑i13 (FSSAI) allergen disclaimer, CoA reference number, ISO/GMP logos.	Conforms to BIS IS 3024 (labeling of essential oils) and destination‑country labeling laws.
Barcoding / RFID	GS1‑128 linear barcode on each carton linking to batch record.	Enables traceability through customs and client’s warehouse management system (WMS).
6. Export Documentation – The “Paper Trail”
Document	Issuing Authority	Key Fields / Notes
Commercial Invoice	Exporter	Currency (USD/EUR), INCOTERMS (usually FOB/Ramgarh Port or CIF), total value, HS‑code, buyer & seller details, signing authority.
Packing List	Exporter	Detailed description, dimensions, gross/net weight, container numbers.
Certificate of Origin (CO)	Chamber of Commerce (e.g., Madhya Pradesh Chamber)	Preferential origin if exporting under GSP; includes IEC number.
Phytosanitary Certificate	APEDA/Plant Quarantine	Mandatory for plant‑derived products; attests that root material is pest‑free.
Certificate of Analysis (CoA) / MSDS	Accredited Lab	Batch‑specific test results; MSDS prepared per GHS (Globally Harmonized System).
Export Declaration (Shipping Bill)	Customs via ICEGATE	Digital filing; contains HS code, value, INCOTERMS, port of export.
Letter of Credit (LC) / Documentary Credit	Issuing Bank (buyer)	Must match invoice details; includes “clean” CoA, MSDS, and packing list.
Insurance Certificate	Marine insurer (e.g., J.C. Baker)	Coverage for All Risks (CIF) – includes loss/damage, spoilage, delayed delivery.
FSSAI Import Permit (for Food/Flavor use)	FSSAI	Required when product is to be used as a flavoring agent in food.
REACH / EU Cosmetics Notification (if EU)	ECHA	Submission of EU‑Cosmetics‑Notification (CPNP) number; keep proof on file.
Digital Tip: Use E‑Filing portals (ICEGATE, APEDA e‑Permit) and store PDFs in a cloud‑based DMS with version control.

7. Customs Clearance & Freight Forwarding
Pre‑Shipping:

Upload Shipping Bill on ICEGATE → obtain Bill of Export (BE) number.
Coordinate with freight forwarder for container booking (20‑ft or 40‑ft HC).
Incoterms Selection:

FOB (Free on Board) – most common for bulk shipments; buyer assumes freight & insurance.
CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) – seller takes responsibility up to destination port (useful for new buyers).
Container Loading & Sealing:

Use ISO‑Containers compliant with ISPM 15 (heat‑treated wood).
Sealed by Customs; retain seal number on packing list.
Customs Inspection (India):

CED (Customs Examination Desk) may random‑check for compliance (e.g., hazardous material).
Present CoA and MSDS on request.
Export Clearance:

Pay IGST (if applicable) on export; claim refund under GST Export Scheme.
Export Declaration cleared → generate Electronic Export Manifest (EEM).
In‑Transit Monitoring:

GPS tracking of container; share live position with buyer.
For temperature‑sensitive shipments (e.g., high‑value boutique blends) use refrigerated reefers (2‑8 °C) and data loggers.
Port of Destination Clearance:

Buyer (or appointed customs broker) presents Bill of Lading, Commercial Invoice, CoA, MSDS, and REACH/FTI certificates.
Pay import duties (often nil for essential oils under HS 3301) and any VAT/GST.
