Lemongrass (Cymbopogon flexuosus / C. martini) Essential‑Oil Export Guide – India
“From field to fragrance house” – a complete, step‑by‑step play‑book for Indian exporters

Target audience – Small‑/medium‑scale growers, contract farmers, distilleries, and export‑houses that already have a steam‑distillation plant (or are planning to contract a third‑party processor).

Scope – All critical phases: agronomic sourcing → harvest → oil extraction → quality assurance → export‑compliance → freight → client after‑sales support.

1. Export‑Readiness Foundations
Requirement	Why it matters	How to obtain / implement
IEC (Import‑Export Code)	Mandatory for any foreign‑trade transaction.	Apply on DGFT – IEC portal (free, 2‑3 days).
APEDA Registration	Gives access to export incentives, market‑development assistance, organic/halal certification schemes.	Submit PAN, GST, IEC, bank details on APEDA portal; obtain APEDA‑Export Licence.
Bank LC Facility	Guarantees payment and mitigates foreign‑exchange risk.	Open an Export‑Advise Letter of Credit with your bank; negotiate sight/Usance terms.
HS Code	Determines duty & statistical reporting.	HS 3301.10.00 – Lemongrass (Cymbopogon) essential oil.
Regulatory Matrix	Destination markets (EU, US, GCC, Japan, Korea, Australia) have differing cosmetics/food‑flavour rules (REACH, FDA‑CFR, EU Cosmetic Regulation, etc.).	Build a spreadsheet: market → regulator → required docs (CoA, REACH registration, safety dossier, halal/organic). Review quarterly.
Organic / Fair‑Trade / Halal Certification (optional but premium)	Enables higher price points in natural‑ingredients segment.	Register with APEDA‑Organic, India GAP, Halal Certification Bodies (JAKIM, Halal‑India) or private certifiers (e.g., Ecocert).
Cargo Insurance (All‑Risks)	Covers loss, spoilage, or delay in transit.	Purchase a marine cargo policy (CIF basis) from a reputable insurer (e.g., JCB Insurance, Tata AIG).
2. Cultivation & Harvesting of Lemongrass
Step	Technical Detail	Best‑Practice Checklist
Cultivar	Cymbopogon flexuosus ‘Mysore’ (high citral ≈ 75 %) or C. martini ‘Coimbatore’ (high geraniol).	Obtain certified planting material from ICAR‑NBRI or APEDA‑approved nurseries.
Climate & Soil	Warm tropical climate; annual rainfall 800‑1500 mm; well‑drained sandy‑loam, pH 5.5‑6.8.	Conduct soil analysis; amend with well‑decomposed farmyard manure (5 t ha⁻¹) + NPK (80‑40‑60 kg ha⁻¹).
Irrigation	Drip system, 0.6‑0.8 L m⁻² day⁻¹ after establishment; reduce after 90 days.	Install soil‑moisture sensors for water‑use efficiency.
Pest & Disease Management	IPM – use Trichoderma spp., Neem‑based formulations, avoid systemic fungicides (they may contaminate oil).	Maintain a pesticide‑use log; ensure residues stay below FSSAI/REACH limits.
Planting Density	45,000‑55,000 plants ha⁻¹ (spacing 75 cm × 70 cm).	Record field layout; GIS‑tag plots for traceability.
Maturity & Yield	Harvest 90‑120 days after planting; each plant yields 1.5‑2 kg fresh stalks; essential‑oil content 0.5‑1.2 % (w/w). Typical fresh‑stalk yield 12‑15 t ha⁻¹.	Keep harvest logs; target ≥ 0.8 % oil content (wet basis).
Harvest Technique	Cut stalks 5‑10 cm above ground, preferably in the early morning (06:00‑08:00) when citral concentration peaks.	Use sharp, sterilised machetes; collect in ventilated, UV‑protected crates to avoid wilting.
Post‑Harvest Handling	Transport to distillery within 2 h; keep stalks in cool shade (≤ 25 °C), avoid direct sunlight to prevent hydrolysis of citral.	Record time‑in‑transit; maintain a temperature log.
Traceability	Assign Batch‑Lot ID: LEM‑IN‑YYMM‑NN; QR‑code on each crate, GPS‑tagged field.	Integrated with ERP for downstream traceability.
3. Processing – Steam Distillation (Oil Production)
Process	Critical Parameters	Control / QC Points
Pre‑Distillation Conditioning	Trim stalks to 30‑40 cm; wash with potable water; air‑dry 1‑2 h to reach ≈ 70 % moisture (reduces water‑load in boiler).	Moisture meter; ambient RH log.
Distillation Method	Steam distillation in a stainless‑steel closed‑loop still (AISI‑304), single‑stage or continuous‑type.	
Boiler Pressure	0.6‑0.9 MPa (≈ 6‑9 bar) – ensures adequate steam generation without overheating the oil.	Calibrated pressure transducer.
Steam Temperature	100‑110 °C (steam temperature at the point of entry).	Thermocouples at inlet & outlet.
Steam‑to‑Biomass Ratio	3‑4 kg steam kg⁻¹ fresh stalk (optimised to avoid hydro‑thermal degradation of citral).	Flow‑meter on steam line.
Distillation Time	3‑4 h per batch (typical 150‑200 kg fresh stalk per run).	Timer; record start/stop times.
Condensate Separation	Use a continuous separator (gravity‑separator) operating at 10‑15 °C. Oil floats (density 0.86‑0.88 g cm⁻³) over aqueous phase.	Level sensors; automatic oil‑draw‑off valve.
Oil Yield Benchmark	0.5‑1.2 % w/w from fresh stalks (average 0.85 %).	Compare batch yield against target; flag deviations > 15 %.
Solvent‑Free	No organic solvent; reduces regulatory burden (no residual‑solvent testing).	Validate absence of solvents by GC‑FID.
Post‑Distillation Storage	Store oil in amber‑glass (or stainless‑steel with nitrogen blanketing) at 15‑20 °C, away from light.	Nitrogen purge (≥ 95 % N₂); UV‑protected storage.
Safety Note – Operate in explosion‑proof rooms, wear anti‑static PPE, install flash‑point detectors and automatic fire‑suppression (CO₂).

4. Laboratory Quality Assurance & Certification
Test	Standard / Method	Acceptance Criteria
GC‑MS Fingerprint	ISO 11024‑1 (Essential oils – GC‑MS).	Major markers: Citral (geranial + neral) ≥ 70 %, Linalool ≤ 5 %, α‑Terpineol ≤ 2 %, β‑Myrcene ≤ 1 %.
Optical Rotation	ISO 9366‑1.	[α]D²⁰ = +30 ± 3° (typical range).
Specific Gravity	ISO 4169.	0.86‑0.88 at 20 °C.
Acid Value (AV)	ISO 12966‑2.	AV ≤ 4 mg KOH g⁻¹.
Peroxide Value (PV)	ISO 3960.	PV ≤ 10 meq O₂ kg⁻¹.
Citral Isomer Ratio (geranial : neral)	Chiral GC‑FID.	Geranial : neral ≈ 55 : 45 (± 5 %).
Microbial Load	ISO 20776‑1.	≤ 10³ CFU g⁻¹.
Heavy Metals (Pb, Cd, As, Hg)	ISO 17294‑2 (ICP‑MS).	Pb ≤ 0.1 ppm, Cd ≤ 0.05 ppm, As ≤ 0.2 ppm, Hg ≤ 0.01 ppm.
Pesticide Residues	FSSAI & EU MRLs (LC‑MS/MS).	All below legislative limits (e.g., λ‑Cyhalothrin ≤ 0.02 ppm).
Certificates	Certificate of Analysis (CoA), Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS), optional Organic/ISO‑9001/ISO‑14001.	Issued by NABL‑accredited lab (e.g., CSIR‑IICT, NIPRD). Include full GC‑MS chromatogram, citral isomer ratio, and residual‑solvent (none).
Digital QA Dashboard – Connect LIMS to ERP so CoA fields auto‑populate on batch release, reducing manual transcription errors.

5. Packaging, Preservation & Labelling
Requirement	Specification	Rationale
Primary Container	Amber‑glass bottles (30 ml, 100 ml, 250 ml) with PTFE‑lined screw caps; HDPE/PP bulk drums (20 L‑200 L) with nitrogen‑purged inner liners for large shipments.	UV protection, oxidation barrier; complies with ISO 16128‑1 for natural‑cosmetic ingredients.
Secondary Packaging	Four‑wall corrugated cartons, inner foam inserts, sealed in a nitrogen‑flushed poly‑bag.	Shock‑absorption, moisture barrier for sea/road transport.
Atmosphere Control	Nitrogen purge (≥ 95 % N₂) before sealing; include silica‑gel packets (1 % w/w).	Extends shelf‑life (≥ 24 months) by preventing oxidation of citral.
Label Content (per market)	Product name, batch‑lot, net weight, production & expiry dates, CoA reference, IN‑PD‑i13 allergen disclaimer, ISO / APEDA / Organic / Halal logos, QR‑code linking to traceability page.	Aligns with BIS IS 3024, EU Regulation 1223/2009, US CFR 21.
Barcoding / RFID	GS1‑128 linear barcode on each carton + optional UHF RFID tag.	Enables fast customs scanning and buyer WMS integration.
Safety Data	MSDS prepared per GHS (pictograms, signal word, hazard statements).	Mandatory for customs & client safety teams.

Document	Issuer	Key Fields / Remarks

Packing List	Exporter	Detailed description, dimensions, gross/net weight, container numbers, seal numbers.
Certificate of Origin (CO)	Chamber of Commerce (e.g., Delhi Chamber)	Must include IEC, APEDA licence; note GSP if preferential duty applies.
Phytosanitary Certificate	APEDA / Plant Quarantine	Confirms raw material (stalks) is pest‑free.

Export Declaration (Shipping Bill)	Customs via ICEGATE	Digital filing; generates Bill of Export (BE) number.
Letter of Credit (LC)	Buyer’s bank	Must match invoice details; attach CoA & MSDS as required.
Insurance Certificate	Marine insurer (e.g., JCB Insurance)	Covers All Risks (CIF).
FSSAI Import Permit (if for food/flavour)	FSSAI	Required when oil is to be used as a flavoring agent.

Best practice: Store all PDFs in a cloud‑based Document Management System (DMS) with version control; grant read‑only access to buyer, freight forwarder, and customs brokers.

7. Customs Clearance & Freight Forwarding
Pre‑Shipping (India)

Upload Shipping Bill on ICEGATE → obtain BE number.
Book a 20‑ft or 40‑ft HC container with a freight forwarder (e.g., Allcargo, DP World, Kuehne & Nagel).
Ensure container complies with ISPM‑15 (heat‑treated wood) for international shipments.
Incoterms Selection

FOB – most common; seller’s responsibility ends at Ramgarh/Port of Kolkata.
CIF – seller covers freight & insurance; useful for new or bulk buyers.
Loading & Sealing

Load cartons on pallets (max height 1.2 m), apply seal number on loading list.
Capture high‑resolution photos of the loaded container for proof of condition.
Customs Examination (India)

Random inspections may be triggered. Keep CoA, MSDS, Phytosanitary Cert ready for officer review.
Export Clearance

Pay IGST (if applicable) and claim refund under the GST Export Scheme.
Generate Electronic Export Manifest (EEM) for port authority.
In‑Transit Monitoring

Install a GPS tracker on the container; optional temperature/humidity datalogger (maintain ≤ 25 °C).
Share live location & condition data with the buyer via a client portal.
Port‑of‑Destination Clearance

Buyer (or appointed customs broker) presents Bill of Lading, Invoice, CoA, MSDS, and any REACH/CPNP documentation.
Pay any import duty (generally nil for HS 3301.10) plus local VAT/GST.
Delivery to Client

Arrange door‑to‑door or warehouse delivery per buyer terms (DAP, DDP).

Final Thought
Exporting Lemongrass Essential Oil from India is a high‑value, highly regulated activity. Success hinges on integrated traceability (field → batch), precise steam‑distillation control, robust analytical certification, and flawless export documentation. By institutionalising the steps above—especially the quality‑first mindset, digital paperwork, and real‑time logistics visibility—you will meet the exacting standards of global fragrance houses, perfume manufacturers, and natural‑ingredients buyers, while protecting the reputation of Indian lemongrass‑derived premium aromas on the world stage.

Stay compliant, stay consistent, and let the fresh, citrusy scent of Indian lemongrass travel safely across borders.
